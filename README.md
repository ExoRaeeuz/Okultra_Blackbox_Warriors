# Breaking the Black Box

**Matrix decompositions by hand vs. NumPy/SciPy**
OKULTRA's Blackbox Warriors (Group 18) · Raymond Morrow & Alan Valencia · Computational Math 300, WSU Tri-Cities, Fall 2026

[![Open In Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/assets/colab-badge.svg)](https://colab.research.google.com/github/ExoRaeeuz/Okultra_Blackbox_Warriors/blob/main/notebooks/blackbox_warriors.ipynb)

We implement Householder QR and LU with partial pivoting from scratch, check them against the LAPACK routines behind `numpy.linalg.qr` and `scipy.linalg.lu`, and stress-test both on ill-conditioned and adversarial matrices. As a bonus, we add classical and modified Gram–Schmidt to show why Householder is the method libraries actually use.

## Conventions

- All arithmetic is `float64`, and no function modifies its input.
- **QR** is the *reduced* factorization: for an m×n A with m ≥ n, Q is m×n with orthonormal columns and R is n×n upper triangular. Q and R are unique only up to column signs, so we normalize to `diag(R) > 0` before comparing entries.
- **LU** returns `P, L, U` with **PA = LU**, where L is unit lower triangular. SciPy returns `A = P_s L U`, so our `P` is SciPy's `P_s.T`.

## Results

**Validation.** All implemented methods reconstruct A to within ~1e-15 relative error on every test matrix and reproduce the Exam 2 hand answers exactly (Q3 for QR, Q2 for PA = LU).

**Loss of orthogonality** (`figures/orthogonality_hilbert.pdf`). On Hilbert matrices, Householder keeps ‖QᵀQ − I‖ at machine precision regardless of conditioning, MGS degrades like κ·ε, and CGS degrades like κ²·ε until orthogonality is lost entirely:

| n | κ₂(Hₙ) | NumPy | Householder | MGS | CGS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 1.6e+04 | 6.6e-16 | 1.1e-15 | 3.0e-13 | 3.5e-11 |
| 8 | 1.5e+10 | 4.7e-16 | 7.3e-16 | 2.6e-07 | 1.0e+00 |
| 12 | 1.6e+16 | 1.1e-15 | 1.2e-15 | 1.1e-01 | 5.0e+00 |

Even when CGS's Q is not orthogonal at all, ‖A − QR‖ stays near 1e-16. Small backward error does not guarantee a useful factorization.

**Pivoting** (`figures/growth_wilkinson.pdf`). Without pivoting, LU on `[[ε, 1], [1, 1]]` divides by ε and loses all accuracy as ε → 0, while partial pivoting stays exact. Partial pivoting can still be beaten: the Wilkinson matrix reaches the worst-case growth factor 2ⁿ⁻¹.

**Conditioning** (`figures/forward_error_hilbert.pdf`). Solving Hₙx = b, every stable method's forward error tracks κ·ε. The error comes from the problem, not the algorithm.

**Timing** (`figures/timing.pdf`). Every method scales as O(n³), but LAPACK's compiled, blocked routines are orders of magnitude faster than our Python loops.
